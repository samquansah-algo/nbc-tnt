Turn intelligence into capability.

    What we're building

    Infrastructure, not a single product.

    NBC combines programmable physical experiences, machine intelligence and evidence from meaningful action to help children develop capabilities they can carry into unfamiliar situations. Each part has to earn its place with evidence before it expands.

    1 · Experience

    Physical experiences you can reprogramme

    The same objects take different roles, rules and responses, so one kit supports many challenges.

    2 · Intelligence

    Machine intelligence, with people in charge

    It helps create, translate and prepare activities and gives bounded feedback. A person reviews it. No automated scoring or routing yet.

    3 · Evidence

    Evidence from what children do

    What a child tried, under what conditions, with what help, and how sure we are.

    4 · Capability

    Capability that holds

    Shown again when the task changes. That's transfer, and it's what we're testing.

    The architecture

    Seven parts, each behind a gate.

    We build the simplest version first and add complexity only when it earns its cost. A dashed gate means "later".

    Institutional portability: making valuable learning routines easier to reproduce, so every site doesn't have to recreate an expensive institution. People, local judgement and care stay essential.

    Capability, broadly

    Starting narrow, on purpose.

    Capability includes reasoning, making, collaboration, care, inquiry, adaptation and participation, and foundational knowledge is part of it. We start with one bounded family for ages about 9–12: changing one variable at a time. There, transfer can be tested and a fair test can be judged.

    Reasoning · first family: control of variablesMakingCollaborationCareInquiryAdaptationParticipation

    Later families need their own task design, review and evidence before we claim anything about them.

    Global by design

    Scarcity and abundance.

    The next billion children grow up in technology-scarce and technology-rich places: homes, schools, libraries, museums, community programmes and makerspaces. We test both conditions with comparable ages before interpreting any difference.

    Cape Coast, Ghana · development base

    Limited individual device access

    Does the experience enable meaningful practice, and later use of the idea?

    Contrasting site · to recruit

    Extensive AI access, same age

    What understanding survives when optional AI help changes or is withdrawn?

    The contrasting site is a recruitment objective, not a partnership. A small comparison can identify failure modes; it can't establish worldwide validity. Necessary assistive technology always stays in place.

    Learning Worlds · working name · the first offer

    Three worlds. One capability.

    A Learning World is a reusable kit, a set of challenge cards and a facilitator plan. The first three look deliberately different but exercise the same idea, changing one thing at a time. That way we can see whether a child carries the idea from one world to another.

    Ramp World · ready for pilots

    Make the car go farther.

    Height, surface and start. Forces and fair tests.

    Plant World · in design

    Which one grows taller?

    Light, water and soil over two weeks.

    Swing World · in design

    Make it swing slower.

    Length, weight and push.

    Other capability families (making, collaboration, care, inquiry, adaptation, participation) come later, each with its own task design and evidence.

    What's in the Ramp World kit

    Things you can find anywhere.

    Chosen so a programme can replace anything that breaks from a local market. Instrument only what a real need justifies.

    • 2 folding card ramps, 60 cm
    • 1 toy car, the same one every run
    • Books or blocks of similar thickness
    • 3 surface mats: carpet, card, tile
    • A tape measure in centimetres
    • 6 challenge cards with printed tokens
    A 45-minute session

    Clear enough to run on day one.

    MinutesChildrenFacilitator
    00–05Hear the challenge; predict and say whyReads the card; notes predictions
    05–15Build the ramp; run 1 and measureExplicit instruction: what a fair test is
    15–30Change one thing; run again; compareOne tap per child: what they changed and with how much help
    30–40Explain: "How do you know?"Asks the probe questions on the card
    40–45Tidy up; one sentence to take homeConfirms notes; checks the suggested next activity

    Explicit instruction plus probe questions, not discovery alone. That's where published evidence for this capability is strongest.

    Transfer

    Does it hold when the world changes?

    Weeks later, children meet a world they haven't seen, with no prompts. If they still change one thing and explain why, the idea may have transferred. These tasks stay private and are scored by someone who didn't teach the child.

    For programme operators and funders

    Run meaningful practical learning.

    You run hands-on sessions every week, and funders and families ask what children are learning. NBC gives you reusable experiences, clear facilitator guidance and a concise evidence summary that helps choose each child's next step.

    Kits

    A Learning World for up to six children per kit, from low-cost, replaceable materials.

    Facilitator guidance

    Two short training sessions, a printed card per challenge, and human support.

    An offline app

    One tap per child. Suggestions from the assistant are always checked by your facilitator.

    An evidence summary

    What children tried, with what help, and what to offer next. There are no scores or rankings.

    The pilot

    Eight weeks. One world.

    1. Week 0: ethics, consent, safeguarding and set-up
    2. Weeks 1–2: facilitator training and first sessions
    3. Weeks 3–6: sessions run by your facilitators
    4. Week 7: a new, unfamiliar task, scored independently
    5. Week 8: your evidence summary and a decision together

    Price: set with each pilot site from a full cost sheet. We'd rather tell you the real cost than quote a number that doesn't hold.

      For families

      See how your child takes on a challenge.

      After each Learning World, you get a short update: what your child tried, their own words, and one question to ask at home. No grades, no rankings, no comparisons with other children.

      Questions families ask
      Who can see my child's record?

      You, your child's facilitator and programme. NBC staff see it only to run and improve the programme, within the consent you give.

      Can I correct it?

      Yes. If something in the record is wrong or missing context (language, help your child had, a bad day), tap "Correct this record" and a person will review it.

      Is my child's data sold or used to select children?

      No. The record is not the product. It is never sold, never used for advertising, and never used to select, sort or rank children.

      How long is it kept?

      Only as long as its purpose (choosing your child's next learning step) needs, then it's deleted. You can ask for deletion at any time.

      Why no scores?

      We describe what your child did, under what conditions and with how much help, because that tells you more than a number, and it can be wrong in ways you can see.

      Learning before measurement

      Four separate questions.

      An attractive activity, a reliable assessment and an effective learning intervention are different achievements. Each needs its own evidence.

      1 · Engage

      Can children meaningfully engage?

      2 · Develop

      Does it help develop a defined capability?

      3 · Interpret

      Does the evidence support a bounded interpretation?

      4 · Deliver

      Can people beyond the founding team deliver it sustainably?

      What a record looks like

      Observed, inferred, predicted: kept apart.

      Every record states conditions and uncertainty, and can be corrected. A group success is never counted as one child's mastery. There's no general intelligence score and no ranking.

      Method note

      The first task family is control of variables for ages 9–12, where published evidence shows children can learn and transfer the idea with explicit instruction plus probe questions. Claims will state how many tasks they rest on. Assessment tasks are held out; they never appear in demos, marketing or Show-Me Days. Thresholds are set before testing, and "inconclusive" is a valid result.

      Ethics and consent

      No data is collected before ethics review, parental consent and child assent are in place. Children's data is never sold or used for selection. Retention follows a specific purpose. Families can see and correct records. Necessary assistive technology is kept and recorded, never treated as help.

      We'll publish what we find, including what doesn't work.

      For investors and funders

      Answers are getting cheaper. Capability isn't.

      Access to useful machine intelligence keeps expanding, unevenly. What remains costly is the chance for children to investigate, create, revise and show what they can do when circumstances change. NBC is building globally relevant learning infrastructure for that, through a focused, testable first offer.

      Long-term direction

      The Human Capability Graph

      A record of what a learner has demonstrated, under which conditions, with what help and with what uncertainty. It is a research and technical concept, earned through evidence. It is not a product today and not a credential.

      First offer

      Learning Worlds for programme operators

      Reusable physical experiences, facilitator guidance and an evidence summary, for recurring hands-on programmes with children aged about 9–12, starting in Cape Coast.

      Economics

      Three separate accounts.

      They're kept apart so no speculative revenue can rescue a weak learning business. Prices come from a full cost sheet and pilot logs; earlier model outputs are historical assumptions.

      Core

      Learning service

      Operator or site fee. A family subscription through the existing LMS is a separate test.

      Option

      Shared intelligence service

      Contracted useful workloads for operators or communities, only with repeat paid demand.

      Option

      Creator and infrastructure services

      Licensed experience packages and implementation, if rights-cleared and wanted.

      The downside case has zero utility or contribution revenue. Child-data sales, tokens, assumed royalties and marketplace proceeds are excluded from the business case.

      Key risks, stated plainly

      • Substitution: ordinary materials plus expert facilitation may achieve the same learning more cheaply. Then we simplify to the useful part.
      • Transfer may not appear, or may be too costly to measure credibly.
      • Operators may value the programme but not pay enough to cover delivery.
      • Automated capability scoring could lose institutional trust. That's why records stay human-readable and the system can run without the graph.
      • Research with children in two countries takes time to approve.
      • "NBC" and "Next Billion" have existing uses; naming needs clearance.
