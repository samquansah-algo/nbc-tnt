## The problem

Most children in the world learn at tables, not screens. Ghana now requires every child to learn computing, and most of its schools have few working computers. When a child works something out with their hands, nothing is kept: teachers can't see who is ready for what, and parents get nothing to hold on to. AI has learned from what people wrote, and has almost no record of how people reason with real objects.

## What we're building

Four children share a table. Everything on it is cheap and can be made locally.

- **Stones.** Bottle caps, clay or wood with a stamped code. Under a cent each; a village can make its own.
- **Game cloth.** A printed cloth sets the task. Lay a new cloth to play a new game.
- **Lamp.** A retired phone fixed above the table reads every move. It has no microphone and keeps no images.
- **School AI.** Runs on the staff's own phones, keeps each child's skill record, and picks the next game for each child.

Children pass a **turn stone**, so every move has an owner without any camera on a face. A child's record says things like "changes one thing at a time" or "predicts, then checks". When the same skill shows up in a different game, the record shows that it carried over.

## Who pays

1. **Schools and after-school clubs:** kits, a yearly subscription per facilitator, and licences for small independent schools.
2. **AI labs:** tests of physical reasoning built from our games, with results from **paid adult testers**. Children's data is never sold.
3. **Funders and governments (later):** pay for results, per verified skill.

Families always use the table for free.
